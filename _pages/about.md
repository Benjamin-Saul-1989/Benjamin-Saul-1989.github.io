I've spent fifteen years solving complex technology problems at the intersection of development, operations, and security. I started with on-premises Windows and Linux servers, learning systems the hard way — one configuration at a time — and grew with the industry as it moved to cloud.

Along the way I found my real focus: proving that security and speed are not opposites. Too many organizations treat them as a trade-off, slowing teams down in the name of protection. I've spent my career showing the opposite is true — that when security is built into the pipeline rather than bolted on at the end, it makes engineering faster, not slower.

Today I lead Cyber Security, Technical Accounts, and DevOps at Salvo Software, where I rebuilt CI/CD for three engineering teams and tripled their delivery throughput. I also run an independent security practice, helping organizations design and defend hybrid and multi-cloud environments. My work spans penetration testing and threat hunting through to architecture — designing security controls that hold up under real conditions, not just on paper.

Most recently I've taken that same approach into AI. While most organizations were still writing acceptable-use policies, I was building the infrastructure: sandboxed developer environments that isolate AI tooling from the host system, a policy engine that blocks non-compliant requests before they execute, and governance controls that ensure sensitive data never reaches an AI system. Policy that can't be enforced isn't a control — it's a hope. I build controls.

Earlier in my career I worked across five years at UST, progressing from Cloud Architect to Senior Site Reliability Engineer on a platform handling millions of daily transactions. I've modernized legacy systems into microservices, designed disaster recovery with automated failover, reduced cloud spend through disciplined infrastructure work, and maintained SOC 2 and ISO 27001 compliance on systems where downtime wasn't an option.

Outside work, I contribute to the open-source security community by maintaining tooling for radio testing with software-defined radios. I 3D-print prosthetics for children through the Enable the Future project, and I participate in bug bounty programs to keep my offensive skills sharp. My own career was shaped by mentors, so I mentor junior DevSecOps engineers in Mexico City — paying forward what was given to me.

I'm a native English speaker with basic Spanish and Japanese, which is how I've worked with and led teams across three continents. I adapt quickly to different cultures and contexts, and I've learned that the best technical solutions come from listening first.

I'm driven to leave organizations and people better than I found them. Technology is the tool. Human potential is the outcome.
