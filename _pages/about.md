I've spent fifteen years solving complex technology problems at the intersection of development, operations and security. I started with on-premises Windows and Linux servers, learning systems the hard way one configuration at a time, and grew with the industry as it moved to the cloud.

Along the way I found my real focus: proving that security and speed are not opposites. Too many organizations treat them as a trade-off, slowing teams down in the name of protection. I've spent my career showing the opposite is true: when security is built into the pipeline rather than bolted on at the end, it makes engineering faster, not slower.

Today I lead Cyber Security, Technical Accounts and DevOps at Salvo Software, where I rebuilt CI/CD and delivery dashboards for three engineering teams in Mexico, the USA and India and **cut the time between versions from 30 days to 10**. I handle every security incident for the company, and I also run an independent security practice through Affila Technology, helping organizations design and defend hybrid and multi-cloud environments, from penetration testing and threat hunting through to security architecture that holds up under real conditions, not just on paper.

I've taken that same approach into AI. While most organizations were still writing acceptable-use policies, I was building the infrastructure: a sandboxed development environment for **50 developers** that isolates AI tooling from the host system and from sensitive repositories, local models running on company hardware, a fully logged backend for every AI request, and governance controls that keep sensitive data away from AI. Policy that can't be enforced isn't a control, it's a hope. I build controls.

Before Salvo, at UST, I worked on Wolters Kluwer's SureTax, a tax engine processing millions of calculations a day, where I **cut mean time to recovery by 65%**, **cut mean time to remediation by 70%** and **reduced Azure costs by 30%, then a further 25%**, while keeping the platform SOC 2 and ISO 27001 compliant. Earlier, on AvidXchange's payments platform, I led the enterprise monitoring work that took recurring service failures from roughly one every three days to a handful a year, and moved 18 legacy applications to Kubernetes. In between, at Avertium, I secured cloud environments for 13 managed security clients and traced a live holiday intrusion to its entry point across 12 sites so the team could evict the attackers.

Outside work, I teach. Every second Saturday I run a cybersecurity class in the morning and a self-hosting class in the afternoon at FAC, a non-profit in Mexico City, in English and Spanish. I build things too: open-source tools like Linux Sync, software-defined radio projects, a PCB business card that detects passing aircraft, and 3D-printed prosthetics for children through Enable the Future. My own career was shaped by mentors, so I mentor junior DevSecOps engineers in Mexico City, paying forward what was given to me.

I'm a native English speaker from Australia with conversational Spanish and elementary Japanese, and I've called Mexico home for thirteen years. I've worked with and led teams across three continents, and I've learned that the best technical solutions come from listening first.

I'm driven to leave organizations and people better than I found them. Technology is the tool. Human potential is the outcome.
